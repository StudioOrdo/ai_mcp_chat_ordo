# Local flyers

Print your QR where your neighbors already look. This guide shows
how to pick two honest spots, print cleanly, and read what comes
back so the signal is not wasted.

## Why this preset exists

Your immediate neighborhood is a surprisingly good test surface for
a beginner solopreneur. The people walking past a coffee shop
bulletin board are not strangers on the internet — they are people
with the same zip code, similar problems, and a very low cost to
reach. If your offer cannot pull attention from them, it is unlikely
to pull it from a colder paid audience.

## How to run it

1. Download your QR code from the referrals workspace.
2. Pick two physical spots with foot traffic you already trust — a
   coffee shop bulletin board, a neighborhood gym, a community
   library, or a shared workspace. Two is enough to start; a third
   can come later.
3. Print a small, honest flyer. One sentence of what you do, the QR,
   and nothing else. Your name is fine; a logo is not required.
4. Put them up on Monday so they have the week to work.
5. On Friday, review the introductions that came in. Note which spot
   produced them — that is the signal you are paying for.

## What to learn from it

Flyers are slow and that is the point. Each introduction tells you
three things at once: where it came from, how urgently the visitor
acted, and what the offer looks like to a stranger who found you in
physical space. Treat the week's returns as one data point per spot,
not as conversions. The goal is to decide whether to keep printing
or to graduate to lightweight paid outreach.

## Honest limits

Local flyers do not scale and they are easy to get wrong. Two honest
spots beat twenty random ones; busy does not mean relevant. If a
week produces zero introductions, swap one of the spots — do not add
a third until the first two have each produced at least one.
